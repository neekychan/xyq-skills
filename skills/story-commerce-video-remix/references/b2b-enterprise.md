# B2B / Enterprise / High-Consideration Products

Use for enterprise software, industrial products, operations tools, finance/legal/HR platforms, procurement-heavy products, professional equipment, and high-price/high-risk purchases.

## Buying Psychology

- Buyers care about risk reduction, ROI, compliance, reliability, accountability, integration, professional credibility, and avoiding costly mistakes.
- Drama should come from operational pressure, not consumer-style exaggeration.
- The product proof must be credible: numbers, workflow standardization, case comparison, audit trail, expert process, fewer errors.

## Best Formulas

- **Expert Backing**: show the standard way professionals solve the problem.
- **Efficiency Crush**: compare old process cost, headcount, delay, or error rate to the new process.
- **Status Reversal**: a team underestimated an operational risk; the product exposes or prevents it.
- **Professional Nanny Tutorial**: useful for showing onboarding or process clarity without sounding childish.

## Proof Forms

- Dashboard metric, audit log, reduced handoff, fewer approvals, faster close, lower error rate, incident avoided, procurement checklist, case wall.
- Stakeholder reaction: CFO, ops lead, compliance, account manager, customer success, frontline worker.

## Hook Patterns

- "这次返工不是设计问题，是流程里少了一个检查点。"
- "老板以为只是慢，财务看完报表沉默了。"
- "客户只问了一句话：这个风险谁负责？"
- "他们不是缺人，是每一步都在重复确认。"

## Example Routes

- Operations SaaS: a shipment delay looks like one employee's mistake; the product trace shows the real bottleneck and prevents the next failure.
- Finance tool: a manager almost approves a bad decision; the dashboard surfaces the hidden cost in time.
- Professional equipment: a technician uses the product during a high-pressure service call; the proof is fewer steps and no second visit.

## Risks

- Do not oversimplify complex enterprise buying into a one-person impulse buy.
- Avoid unverifiable ROI numbers unless provided.
- Keep humor restrained; credibility matters more than viral absurdity for risk-sensitive buyers.
