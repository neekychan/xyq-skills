# Shot Logic And Continuity

Use this reference when a brand-film route needs to become a script, storyboard, or Seedance 2.0 prompt. The goal is to make every abstract claim filmable and to make 15 seconds feel like one piece of motion rather than static cards.

## Make Claims Filmable

Any abstract brand claim must become camera-verifiable evidence before it becomes a `Video Prompt`.

Use this chain for every major idea:

**subject -> concrete scene -> pressure/desire -> action -> visible change -> reaction -> theme proof**

- **Subject**: who carries the claim, defined as a role with visible anchors.
- **Concrete scene**: where the pressure can be seen, with physical objects, light, sound, and spatial context.
- **Pressure/desire**: what is missing, blocked, feared, wanted, or at stake.
- **Action**: what the subject physically does, chooses, holds, opens, gives, starts, stops, reveals, or completes.
- **Visible change**: what the camera can see change in the world, object, body, screen, relationship, environment, or result.
- **Reaction**: how the subject or witness visibly registers the change.
- **Theme proof**: how this specific change proves the brand idea without explanatory copy.

For group, montage, city, or "different people" structures, build 2-3 short evidence scenes instead of unbound crowd statements. Extra people can be atmosphere after the key proof scenes are clear.

Convert attitude words into observable behavior. For example, "brave" becomes handing over a hidden work; "free" becomes leaving a queue and moving at one's own rhythm; "healed" becomes two people sharing one object after silence; "seen" becomes a witness stopping to look at the subject's result.

## Motion Spine

A brand film should not read like a list of attractive still frames. Before writing a script or video prompt, define one **motion spine** that makes the 15 seconds feel like one continuous action, rhythm, or emotional transfer.

The motion spine can be:

- **Action relay**: one physical action triggers the next.
- **Sound relay**: one sound becomes the next cut or rhythm point.
- **Object relay**: one object travels, changes hands, changes state, or anchors scenes.
- **Light relay**: light changes from cold to warm, dark to bright, flicker to stable, or screen glow to physical light.
- **Gaze relay**: a subject looks toward the next space, product, person, or proof moment.
- **Space relay**: camera movement, corridor, road, table, room, map, or path carries the viewer.
- **Material relay**: fabric, liquid, paper, smoke, dust, skin, metal, glass, or digital matter transforms across beats.

If the script can be summarized as independent events only, redesign it so each beat hands a visible or audible cue to the next beat.

## Beat Logic

Every timed beat should answer:

- **Narrative function**: what this beat does, such as entering the world, creating pressure, revealing desire, triggering action, proving change, or resolving the brand idea.
- **Transition bridge**: why this beat follows the previous beat, and what visual or sound bridge connects them.
- **Camera grammar**: shot size, angle, camera position, movement, composition, foreground/background relation, and what the viewer notices first.
- **Lighting grammar**: where the key light comes from, whether it is hard or soft, what fill or bounce does, where rim light or reflection appears, and how shadow density supports emotion.
- **Sound logic**: the role of environment sound, human voice, breath, silence, music, phone tone, machine sound, crowd reaction, or object sound.
- **Scene atmosphere**: light, air, texture, crowd density, rhythm, and physical details that make the beat belong to the chosen world.
- **Tone consistency**: a quiet human film should not suddenly become a tech demo; a comedy should build through behavior and timing; a thriller should preserve pressure through sound, light, and blocking.

Before finalizing, check each beat: why this shot now, what changed from the previous shot, how light is designed in this exact frame, and how sound or atmosphere tells Seedance 2.0 how to shoot it.

## Continuity Check

For the whole 15 seconds, identify the motion spine in one sentence, such as `sound cue -> hand action -> object transfer -> light shift -> product proof -> sonic lockup`. The exact device should fit the brand and category.

For each timed beat, check:

- What action, sound, light, object, gaze, space, or material movement from the previous beat pulls the viewer into this beat?
- What cue does this beat leave for the next beat?
- What physically happens inside the shot beyond standing, posing, looking, or holding a product?
- How does the product or brand change the movement pattern, scene state, emotional temperature, or sound rhythm before the lockup?
- Does the final shot close the same visual or sonic loop that opened the film, or intentionally transform it into brand memory?

If the answer is only a plot event, rewrite the beat around a visible action and an audible or visual handoff.
