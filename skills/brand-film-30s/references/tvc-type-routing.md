# Commercial Type Routing

Use this reference before creating brand films, TVCs, campaign films, product promotion films, mood films, and Seedance 2.5 prompts. Pick one primary commercial type / production approach, then let it control visual style, lighting, camera, editing rhythm, sound design, main subject, scene design, and story structure.

Do not treat these as rigid genres. A film may borrow craft from another type, but it needs one clear production approach so the result does not become a generic premium montage.

For each route or prompt, translate the chosen type into production language. Do not output only the type name. Any type-level style description must be concretized through camera language, visual style / art direction, color and material system, lighting behavior, editing rhythm, and sound design.

Before selecting a type, decide whether the film should be live-action, CGI/VFX-led, motion-graphics-led, animated, mixed-media, screen-format, documentary-fragment, or hybrid. The expression form should come from the category tension and brand asset. Real shoot is not always more premium; animation or motion design can be stronger when the product truth is abstract, symbolic, interface-based, or impossible to show literally.

Use formal diversity deliberately:

- **Live-action** for human tension, performance, body language, social proof, product ritual, craft, taste, travel, sport, service friction, or documentary truth.
- **CGI / VFX** for impossible scale, invisible mechanisms, material transformation, future systems, product architecture, sensor logic, beauty science, auto safety, or spectacle.
- **Motion graphics / design animation** for interfaces, data, workflows, platform orchestration, packaging systems, typography, logo worlds, finance, B2B, productivity, or AI processes.
- **Stylized animation / stop-motion** for charm, metaphor, tactile objects, food, family, youth culture, memory, or a deliberately non-real brand world.
- **Mixed media / collage / screen format** for fashion, music, creator tools, fandom, social platforms, youth culture, archives, UGC, and brands whose audience lives across media formats.

The same category can choose different forms depending on the task: a fashion brand may use live-action casting, flash-photo mixed media, or animated garment sculpture; a tech brand may use documentary proof, interface motion graphics, or a cold sci-fi lab; a food brand may use real table warmth, stop-motion tabletop, or surreal appetite comedy.

## Common Commercial Types

### Product Beauty Film / Sensory Product Film

Use when the product's material, shape, texture, interface, scent, taste, or mechanical detail can carry desire by itself.

Production template:

- **When to use**: fragrance, beauty, food, beverage, alcohol, jewelry, electronics, automotive details, packaging, craft, precision, freshness, softness, taste, scent, texture, or premium material.
- **Visual style**: macro detail, controlled studio image, product silhouette, material transformation, liquid/metal/glass/fabric/skin behavior, clean background, C4D or tabletop choreography when useful.
- **Lighting**: hard edge light for silhouette, softbox reflection on glossy surfaces, narrow rim light on glass or metal, top light for texture, water or mirror bounce, controlled specular highlights, deep negative fill for premium contrast.
- **Camera / lens**: macro lens, slow push-in, orbiting product move, rack focus, locked hero frame, extreme close-up, shallow depth of field, match cut between material states.
- **Editing rhythm**: slow and precise; build through contact, reaction, transformation, reveal, lockup. Cuts should feel tactile rather than narrative-heavy.
- **Sound design**: close-mic Foley, liquid pour, cap click, fabric rub, glass tap, fizz, breath, low-frequency pulse, soft whoosh, silence before hero reveal.
- **Main subject**: product, packaging, logo, ingredient, texture, interface, body part touching product, or material sample.
- **Scene design**: studio table, abstract space, dark reflective surface, white cyc, water tank, lab-like clean surface, kitchen/bar/vanity only if the environment adds sensory proof.
- **Story structure**: product starts inert -> light/contact/sound activates the material -> product truth becomes visible -> final silhouette or ritual becomes brand memory.
- **30s beat template**: 0-3s macro sensory hook; 3-8s tactile interaction or material movement; 8-14s product truth starts becoming visible; 14-21s material transformation or usage proof deepens; 21-27s hero pack or ritual resolves desire; 27-30s logo lockup with a memorable sound or light cue.
- **Product / brand role**: the product is the subject and the evidence. It should not be a decorative prop.
- **Prompt requirements**: specify material, light source, reflection, camera distance, product surface behavior, sound motif, and final pack/logo frame.

Common mistakes:

- Product beauty shots with no transformation.
- Macro details that could belong to any premium brand.
- Abstract words replacing physical behavior.

### Spectacle / VFX-Led Commercial

Use when the brand needs instant attention, shareability, cultural scale, or a "how did they do that?" memory.

Production template:

- **When to use**: launches, youth campaigns, sports, automotive, tech, entertainment, fashion, cultural moments, bold brand refreshes, or products that can believably trigger a world-scale visual event.
- **Visual style**: impossible scale, high-risk stunt, surreal spatial event, gravity-defying movement, architectural transformation, split-screen synchronization, mass choreography, weather event, or one single iconic image.
- **Lighting**: motivated cinematic light that proves scale: sun backlight for outdoor bodies, dramatic side light for architecture, hard top light for pressure, large practical sources, storm or stage light, VFX-integrated highlights and shadows.
- **Camera / lens**: wide establishing shot, long take, crane/drone movement, slow reveal, locked proof frame, high-speed shot, tracking shot that follows the impossible event, visible parallax to make scale credible.
- **Editing rhythm**: setup -> anticipation -> spectacle reveal -> proof frame -> simple brand ending. Keep the central event readable.
- **Sound design**: silence before impact, rising music, sub bass, crowd inhale, wind, impact, mechanical movement, spatial whoosh, one iconic music cue.
- **Main subject**: body, vehicle, athlete, crowd, building, natural force, product-controlled space, or one impossible object.
- **Scene design**: real location with strong scale cues, city, stadium, road, apartment, runway, open landscape, architectural space, or studio set designed for one impossible transformation.
- **Story structure**: define a challenge or pressure -> hold tension -> reveal the spectacle -> connect the event to product capability or brand belief -> end with a clean line.
- **30s beat template**: 0-3s impossible or high-stakes setup; 3-8s motion begins and the world starts changing; 8-14s spectacle reaches full form; 14-21s product or brand consequence becomes clear; 21-27s credible proof frame holds; 27-30s brand line or mnemonic lands.
- **Product / brand role**: the brand or product must cause, unlock, measure, or explain the spectacle.
- **Prompt requirements**: specify the impossible event, scale reference, camera position, how the VFX interacts with light/shadow, and the exact proof frame.

Common mistakes:

- Big visuals with no product consequence.
- Spectacle that overshadows the brand.
- VFX that feels arbitrary because it is not tied to a category tension.

### Narrative Lifestyle Film

Use when the brand needs human connection, cultural relevance, romance, warmth, longing, confidence, craft, or an emotionally readable world.

Production template:

- **When to use**: fashion, beauty, food, home, travel, coffee, service platforms, mobile devices, family products, wellness, craft, local lifestyle, and relationship-led categories.
- **Visual style**: lived-in spaces, expressive natural or motivated light, textured interiors, city/night/home/backstage atmosphere, weather, object details, wardrobe that signals character, human-scale framing.
- **Lighting**: window light, practical lamps, soft side light, warm table light, overcast daylight, street reflection, screen glow, controlled fill that protects expression, small rim light on product or face when needed.
- **Camera / lens**: handheld observation, gentle dolly, close-up on hands and faces, over-the-shoulder, motivated pan, eye-line match, shallow depth of field, medium shots that hold body language.
- **Editing rhythm**: scene-based and emotionally legible. Use fewer cuts than a montage; let each beat show a decision, hesitation, contact, exchange, or reaction.
- **Sound design**: room tone, footsteps, fabric, breath, city ambience, quiet dialogue fragment, phone tone, cup sound, door sound, restrained music that supports the emotional turn.
- **Main subject**: a specific protagonist, relationship, ritual, community, worker, creator, family, couple, performer, or wearer.
- **Scene design**: apartment, street, shop, studio, backstage, commute, table, hotel, kitchen, workplace, train, neighborhood, or public space with real objects and human use.
- **Story structure**: introduce pressure or desire -> show a small behavior -> product/brand asset changes the situation -> reaction proves the emotional shift -> final image or line resolves the brand idea.
- **30s beat template**: 0-3s human pressure in a concrete scene; 3-8s protagonist makes a small choice; 8-14s product or brand action changes a relationship, posture, task, or ritual; 14-21s visible reaction proves the emotional shift; 21-27s the new behavior or world holds; 27-30s brand line resolves the idea.
- **Product / brand role**: product is a tool, gift, connector, ritual object, status signal, memory device, or confidence trigger.
- **Prompt requirements**: specify protagonist identity, scene, emotional pressure, product action, visible change, reaction, sound cue, and shot-specific lighting.

Common mistakes:

- Pretty lifestyle scene with no conflict.
- Vague "confidence" or "connection" without a visible behavior.
- Product appears only at the end.

### Mood Film

Use when the brand needs to define a vibe, seasonal world, drop identity, aesthetic territory, or cultural attitude more than a linear story.

Production template:

- **When to use**: fashion drops, fragrance, beauty, music-linked campaigns, art direction films, seasonal campaigns, youth culture, lifestyle, hotels, alcohol, luxury travel, or brand-world building.
- **Visual style**: repeated motifs, rhythmic fragments, bodies, faces, silhouettes, textures, weather, locations, colors, typography, negative space, symbolic objects, styling, sensory montage.
- **Lighting**: one consistent emotional light rule: neon wash, golden hour, overcast softness, flash photography, candlelight, stage light, low-key fashion light, practical lamps, or harsh daylight. Keep the lighting identity coherent across fragments.
- **Camera / lens**: slow glides, still frames, jump cuts, gaze-to-camera, texture inserts, wide empty frames, handheld fragments, lens flare, body close-ups, repeated camera angle or movement.
- **Editing rhythm**: music-first or atmosphere-first. Use recurrence and variation: repeat a color, gesture, location, object, or sound while intensifying the feeling.
- **Sound design**: ambient bed, music loop, synth texture, breath, wind, water, fabric, city hum, repeated object sound, sparse voiceover, or one recurring sonic motif.
- **Main subject**: face, body, silhouette, garment, city, room, landscape, product ritual, object cluster, weather, or seasonal material.
- **Scene design**: a coherent world rather than a plot location: motel room, desert road, rainy city, empty gallery, backstage, club corridor, coastal landscape, studio floor, bedroom, or night street.
- **Story structure**: define one emotional temperature -> introduce recurring motif -> vary the motif through body/material/location -> end on a brand-specific image.
- **30s beat template**: 0-3s mood hook with sound/color/body; 3-8s motif repeats in a second texture or location; 8-14s brand/product code becomes part of the rhythm; 14-21s motif varies through body/material/location; 21-27s the brand world reaches its clearest image; 27-30s final frame fixes the vibe.
- **Product / brand role**: product is a motif, styling rule, material texture, logo behavior, color system, body movement, or final image.
- **Prompt requirements**: specify emotional temperature, recurring motif, color palette, lighting rule, music/sound motif, and final brand-specific frame.

Common mistakes:

- Random pretty fragments.
- Vibe with no brand code.
- Music video energy with no product or category meaning.

### Brand Manifesto / Anthem Film

Use when the brand needs a public stance, cultural leadership, mass inspiration, identity claim, or broad audience inclusion.

Production template:

- **When to use**: sport, auto, public-interest campaigns, brand launches, legacy brand renewal, employer brand, creator platforms, social impact, large communities, or brands with a clear belief.
- **Visual style**: multiple short scenes, diverse people or contexts, documentary or cinematic realism, rhythmic parallel editing, recurring gesture, bold typography, archival texture, split-screen, or large-scale crowd imagery.
- **Lighting**: truthful documentary light for credibility, dramatic backlight for hero moments, stadium or street practicals, dawn/dusk for emotional scale, controlled contrast for the final brand line.
- **Camera / lens**: handheld documentary, long lens observation, match cuts, split screen, close-up reaction, low-angle hero shot, wide crowd shot, fast cutaways tied by motion or voice.
- **Editing rhythm**: voiceover or music builds in waves. Start with pressure, add multiple scenes as evidence, accelerate through repeated gestures, land on one simple brand line.
- **Sound design**: voiceover, percussion, crowd, breath, shoes on ground, training sounds, machine sounds, applause, silence before the final line, rising score.
- **Main subject**: community, athletes, creators, workers, users, citizens, founders, or a cross-section of culture.
- **Scene design**: multiple real-world locations with clear context: field, street, home, office, workshop, school, court, transit, factory, stage, studio, or public square.
- **Story structure**: state pressure or belief -> show several specific evidence scenes -> connect them through one repeated action, word, movement, or edit -> land on the brand belief.
- **30s beat template**: 0-3s belief or pressure stated through one strong scene; 3-8s first evidence scenes expand the world; 8-14s scenes connect through repeated motion/line/music; 14-21s the belief becomes specific through product, platform, or community proof; 21-27s final group or hero frame gathers the emotion; 27-30s brand line lands.
- **Product / brand role**: brand is the belief, platform, community, tool, uniform, event, or cultural organizer. Product can appear as repeated proof but should not interrupt the anthem.
- **Prompt requirements**: specify the belief, 2-3 concrete evidence scenes, recurring edit device, voice/music arc, and final brand line.

Common mistakes:

- Big universal lines with no specific scenes.
- Crowd footage that feels stock.
- Brand claim that could be claimed by any competitor.

### Product Demonstration / Stunt Spot

Use when a product capability can be dramatized through a real or real-feeling test.

Production template:

- **When to use**: automotive, tools, appliances, software with visible output, durability, speed, precision, safety, control, resistance, performance, or category claims that need proof.
- **Visual style**: simple setup, credible test environment, proof frame, visible risk, controlled location, minimal artifice, clear before/after, one memorable stunt when appropriate.
- **Lighting**: practical daylight, workshop light, controlled industrial light, hard light for measurement, clean fill for readability, rim light on product edges, no over-stylization that weakens credibility.
- **Camera / lens**: locked wide shot, long take, slow reveal, side-by-side comparison, close-up of measurement, high-speed detail, overhead test frame, cutaway to product mechanism.
- **Editing rhythm**: test logic first: setup -> condition -> action -> result -> brand meaning. Keep the proof readable before adding style.
- **Sound design**: restrained music or silence, mechanical sound, impact, engine, click, timer, measurement beep, material strain, release sound, final quiet proof.
- **Main subject**: product, vehicle, machine, performer, tool, test object, environment under pressure, or result.
- **Scene design**: test track, road, workshop, lab, kitchen, construction site, warehouse, studio rig, outdoor challenge, or real-use location.
- **Story structure**: define the test -> show stakes or constraint -> perform the test -> reveal result -> translate result into brand meaning.
- **30s beat template**: 0-3s test condition; 3-8s action begins; 8-14s product mechanism or capability becomes readable; 14-21s result becomes undeniable; 21-27s proof frame holds long enough to trust; 27-30s brand line translates the result.
- **Product / brand role**: product is the thing being tested or the mechanism that makes the result possible.
- **Prompt requirements**: specify test condition, camera proof angle, product mechanism, result, measurement or before/after, and final proof frame.

Common mistakes:

- Fake-feeling stunt with no credible setup.
- Test proves a feature but has no emotional memory.
- Celebrity becomes more important than the product proof.

### Format-Based Commercial / Parody Spot

Use when the brand wants cultural wit, modernity, satire, or memorability by adopting a familiar media format.

Production template:

- **When to use**: category critique, modern social behavior, comedy, youth campaigns, tech, fashion, entertainment, food delivery, finance, service platforms, and brands that can credibly play with media language.
- **Visual style**: recognizable media grammar: news broadcast, screen test, product tutorial, reality show, corporate video, public announcement, dating profile, security feed, auction, weather report, interview, trailer, livestream, or social feed.
- **Lighting**: format-accurate lighting first: broadcast key light, studio panel light, webcam light, fluorescent office light, security-camera harshness, flash photography, trailer-style contrast, or reality-show practicals.
- **Camera / lens**: static presenter frame, multi-camera setup, surveillance angle, webcam framing, vertical phone capture, interview close-up, tutorial overhead, trailer montage, deadpan locked-off shot.
- **Editing rhythm**: mimic the chosen format, then let one unexpected brand detail disrupt it. Keep the format recognizable for the first seconds.
- **Sound design**: format-specific cues: news sting, UI beep, room mic, canned applause, tutorial clicks, trailer hits, livestream notification, security hum, presenter voice, awkward silence.
- **Main subject**: person inside the format, product interrupting the format, category ritual reframed by the format, or brand world taking over the format.
- **Scene design**: studio desk, interview room, office training room, phone interface, surveillance corridor, livestream room, fake product demo set, trailer world, or reality-show location.
- **Story structure**: establish familiar format -> introduce one unexpected detail -> brand/product changes the rules -> end with the format transformed or exposed.
- **30s beat template**: 0-3s recognizable format setup; 3-8s wrong or funny detail appears; 8-14s product/brand changes the format logic; 14-21s the format transformation escalates; 21-27s punchline or proof becomes clear; 27-30s brand line lands.
- **Product / brand role**: product or brand must be the reason the format breaks, improves, or reveals a truth.
- **Prompt requirements**: specify exact format, frame layout, lighting style, sound cues, wrong detail, product intervention, and final punchline/line.

Common mistakes:

- Parody with no brand reason.
- Format joke that lasts longer than the idea.
- Weirdness that does not reveal product or audience tension.

## Conversion Grid

When turning research into a route, fill these production dimensions:

- **Commercial type / production approach**: Which common commercial type is primary?
- **Visual style**: Is the film driven by product material, spectacle, lived-in environment, mood fragments, community scenes, product proof, or media format?
- **Style craft translation**: What are the camera language, visual/art-direction rule, color/material system, and sound motif that make this style visible and audible?
- **Lighting**: What is the full-film lighting rule, and how does it shift across the film?
- **Camera / lens**: What camera position, lens behavior, movement, and shot size make the type recognizable?
- **Editing rhythm**: How does the film pace itself: transformation, reveal, emotional turn, motif variation, anthem build, test result, or punchline?
- **Sound design**: What recurring sound, music, ambience, voice, or silence carries the film?
- **Main subject**: What actually carries the film: product, body, face, crowd, vehicle, location, object, format, or relationship?
- **Scene design**: What kind of location or set makes the type legible?
- **Story structure**: Does the film escalate through material transformation, impossible event, human pressure, mood intensification, evidence scenes, test result, or format transformation?
- **Product / brand role**: Does the product cause, prove, connect, test, interrupt, symbolize, or resolve the film?

If the answers point in different directions, choose one primary type and translate the others into supporting production dimensions.

## Case Signals

- **Apple HomePod "Welcome Home"**: narrative lifestyle film plus spectacle / VFX-led commercial. A tired worker returns to a small apartment; music expands the room, so product function becomes spatial and emotional liberation.
- **Nike "You Can't Stop Us"**: brand manifesto / anthem film plus spectacle / VFX-led commercial. Split-screen matching turns many athletes into one continuous body, so editing itself proves unity.
- **Dior J'adore with Rihanna**: luxury fashion film plus product beauty film. Versailles, gold light, celebrity body, and bottle form merge into a coronation ritual.
- **Burberry "Open Spaces"**: spectacle / VFX-led commercial plus mood film. Outdoor flight and gravity-defying movement turn outerwear into a feeling of open freedom.
- **Volvo Trucks "Epic Split"**: product demonstration / stunt spot. A celebrity body becomes the measuring instrument for truck steering precision.
- **Guinness "Surfer"**: cinematic narrative commercial with product analogy. Waiting for a wave mirrors waiting for the pour; horses in foam make patience visceral.
- **Chanel No.5 mini-films**: cinematic narrative commercial plus luxury fashion film. Romance, song, celebrity, and cinematic trailer logic make perfume a touchstone for desire rather than a feature.

Use case signals as production mechanisms only. Do not copy plots, characters, protected scenes, or exact visual identities.
