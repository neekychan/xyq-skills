# Critique Checklist

## Critique Checklist

Use this when reviewing a proposed script:

- Does it target a specific audience and emotion?
- Does it identify a short-drama genre the TA plausibly enjoys?
- Does it choose a content route based on what the product can actually prove?
- Is the first 3 seconds dog-blood enough to stop scrolling through accusation, humiliation, betrayal hint, relationship rupture, public comparison, or absurd misunderstanding?
- Does the hook sound like that genre, not like an ad opening?
- Does the dog-blood opening connect to the product proof later, instead of being empty shock?
- Is there a clear pressure relationship such as boss/employee, ex/current self, mother-in-law/daughter-in-law, husband/wife, rival/protagonist, client/professional, or family/bystander?
- Is the opening an abnormal event, not a product intro?
- Can the viewer understand the relationship pressure in 1-2 seconds?
- Does the conflict escalate through action/reaction rather than explanation?
- If the script is meant to feel "爽", is there a clear antagonist or hostile witness?
- Are the stakes immediate and concrete enough that the protagonist could visibly lose?
- Is the antagonist's wrong judgment specific enough for the payoff to answer it directly?
- Does the script include pressure lock, quiet preparation, visible reversal, and witness reaction?
- Is the product the decisive proof, secret weapon, care object, status signal, wrong-choice correction, or final reveal key?
- If the product were removed, would the protagonist lose or the reveal collapse? If not, the placement is too loose.
- Does the product enable the protagonist's winning move, rather than only making life nicer?
- Is the same product visually locked across the whole video: exact package text, logo position, color, shape, material, cap/lid, package type, quantity, size ratio, and opened/sealed state?
- Does the video avoid wrong-SKU drift: no flavor/color/package swaps, no box-to-bottle changes, no quantity changes without visible add/remove action, and no invented package text?
- Does the video avoid inventing unseen reference-image information: no back view, side panel, ingredients, barcode, inner packaging, internal contents, liquid, powder, food pieces, or accessories unless the user provided them?
- Are all selling points grounded in user-provided text, product title/listing details, hotspot-returned product fields, visible front-package text, or other explicit source material?
- Does the script avoid inventing product proof such as fake ingredient tables, back labels, side panels, certifications, lab reports, specifications, flavor/capacity text, cutaways, or internal contents?
- If a desired selling point is not visually supported by provided assets, does the script adapt by using dialogue, user perception, scene result, or witness reaction instead of fabricating product appearance or package information?
- Does every product movement have a visible physical cause: hand, tool, surface, contact point, movement direction, and endpoint?
- Does the script avoid impossible product behavior: hovering, teleporting, auto-standing, auto-opening, auto-pouring, vanishing, or coming from an implausible body/clothing/food location?
- If the package is opened, torn, poured, worn, clicked, or applied, is that action shown with a plausible grip and continuous state afterward?
- Does the open action match the real package structure: screw cap, flip cap, pull tab, tear notch, zip seal, box flap, shrink film, pump head, sachet edge, can ring, or app click path?
- If the product proof requires inside contents, pouring, eating, applying, taking out, or app activation, is the opening/clicking beat shown before the proof instead of skipped?
- If tearing/opening is fragile or not needed, does the script avoid showing the tear/open action and use an already-opened display, exterior package shot, reaction, result, or dialogue instead?
- After the product is opened, does every later shot keep the opened state consistent: cap removed/flipped, seal broken, tear edge visible, flap open, pump pressed, or app page changed?
- For AB comparison, are the two sides genuinely different: `产品1` as the marketed product and `对照产品A`/`错误选择A` as the old or weaker alternative?
- Does the person who doubted, mocked, or pressured the protagonist witness the reversal?
- Is the proof visible enough to believe?
- Does the video have a clear camera/edit/sound plan matching the selling mode?
- Does the ending create emotional payoff before the conversion line?
- Does at least one dialogue or voiceover line explicitly say the product name, ideally in the final conversion line after the proof lands?
- Is there only one main emotional button?
- Are claims specific and supportable?
- If outputting Seedance 2.0 prompts, does the prompt start with duration/aspect ratio and clearly state generate/reference/edit/extend/stitch?
- Are characters, product, scene, and references bound with stable labels such as `主体1`, `产品1`, `图片1`, and `视频1`?
- Does each Seedance beat start with `拍法`, and does `拍法` clearly specify shot size, angle, composition, camera position, and camera movement before `画面内容`?
- Does each Seedance beat include `画面内容` and `声音`, with the visible proof or payoff written directly at the end of `画面内容` rather than as a separate field?
- Is `商品露出` included only in beats where the product is actually visible or used, with no description of absent, hidden, upcoming, or off-screen product details?
- Does the Seedance prompt include continuity constraints and avoid `--`, vague pronouns, rare characters, and contradictory instructions?
- Does the Seedance prompt include explicit `产品一致性` and `交互物理约束` sentences before the shot list?
