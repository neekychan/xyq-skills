# Originality Checks

Use this reference before finalizing a brand-film route, especially when the idea risks becoming a generic premium montage, ordinary customer story, or interchangeable category ad.

## Brand Fit Test

A normal route is one that could be used by many brands in the category after changing only the logo, product name, or final line. Rewrite the route when two or more answers are weak.

- **Substitution test**: if another brand in the same category can own the same story with no structural change, make the brand asset, product mechanism, material code, or cultural stance more specific.
- **Distinctive setup test**: the opening situation should contain one directed production choice: impossible object, wrong-scale space, strange ritual, social rule, casting contradiction, movement system, sound rule, or visual frame.
- **Visual code test**: the route must have a repeatable rule for framing, light, color, body language, material texture, or scene architecture. "Beautiful", "premium", "cinematic", and "fashionable" are not rules.
- **Behavior test**: the protagonist or subject must do something readable as desire, pressure, refusal, transformation, or proof. Standing, walking, posing, and looking cool are insufficient unless the body language itself is the idea.
- **Product consequence test**: the product or brand asset must change posture, identity, status, world logic, camera point of view, material behavior, or social relation before the logo appears.
- **Memory test**: after one viewing, the idea should leave one specific memory: gesture, object, room, rule, line, silhouette, sound, or transformation.

## Raise The Bar For Fashion And Luxury

For fashion, luxury, streetwear, and trend brands, the story should feel like a designed fashion-world proposition, not a normal life scene with stylish clothes.

Use contradiction, subculture logic, anti-glamour, theatrical casting, surreal everyday rules, atelier/process mythology, body-silhouette transformation, high/low cultural collision, flash photography, screen tests, runway institution, or product ritual. Then translate the proposition into concrete camera language, visual style, color system, lighting, and sound design.

## Modern Brand-Film Checks

- Does the expression form fit the category tension, or is it only fashionable?
- Does AI/CGI/motion design make a product truth visible?
- Does creator-style realism make the brand more credible rather than cheaper?
- Does the first 2-3 seconds create a meaningful hook tied to the brand world?
- Can the hero idea be cut into shorter formats without losing product proof?
- Is there one clear visual, sound, format, or world rule that makes the film recognizable after one viewing?
- Does the ending create a full brand mnemonic: visual lockup, readable logo, spoken brand name when appropriate, spoken slogan / brand line when appropriate, and sonic cue?

## Common Failure Modes

- **Premium montage with no insight**: beautiful but interchangeable.
- **Abstract manifesto**: big words without visible human tension.
- **Product cameo**: product appears but does not change the film.
- **Borrowed issue**: brand uses a social topic it has no credibility to own.
- **Generic road / lab / studio imagery**: category defaults with no new point of view.
- **AI novelty without emotional craft**: technical novelty replaces human feeling.
- **Celebrity overpowers brand**: viewers remember the face, not the brand meaning.
- **Static storyboard list**: beats describe events but do not relay action, sound, light, object, gaze, space, or material.
