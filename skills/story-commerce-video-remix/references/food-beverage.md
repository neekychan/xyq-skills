# Food And Beverage

Use for snacks, drinks, prepared meals, condiments, restaurants, meal kits, supplements positioned as food, regional specialties, and gift food.

## Buying Psychology

- Users buy taste, memory, social sharing, convenience, comfort, freshness, indulgence, health permission, or identity.
- Food proof is sensory and social: first bite, smell, crunch, steam, silence at the table, people hiding or fighting over the last piece.
- Story conflict works best when the product solves mood, relationship, homesickness, social awkwardness, or time pressure.

## Best Formulas

- **Sympathetic Misunderstanding**: someone appears picky, rude, or secretive; the reveal ties the food to memory or care.
- **Dream Fulfillment**: a hometown, childhood, seasonal, or ideal meal becomes reachable.
- **Social Flex**: everyone secretly knows the same snack/drink; the product becomes a small identity badge.
- **Professional Nanny Tutorial**: meal kits, recipes, coffee/tea prep, diet-friendly food.

## Proof Forms

- First-bite reaction, texture close-up, steam, crunch sound, empty plate, repeated reach, guest reaction, smell drawing people in.
- Convenience proof: time from hungry to served, fewer dishes, one-handed prep, office/dorm/travel use.
- Gift proof: recipient expression, packaging reveal, family table sharing.
- Wine / premium beverage proof: visible bottle and label, clean opening ritual, pour close-up, color through the glass, table silence, guest approval, antagonist's expression change.

## Styling For Wine And Banquet Drinks

- If the story pressure is face, gifting, hospitality, family banquet, client dinner, or public judgment of taste, do not dress the protagonist in overly plain homewear. Wardrobe should help the audience believe the protagonist is prepared for the occasion.
- Use restrained but polished styling: black tailored dress with a burgundy silk top, wine-red satin blouse with a black high-waist skirt, subtle pearl or gold accessories, clean makeup, or other dinner-ready looks that echo the wine without stealing attention from it.
- Keep the product dominant: the outfit creates social credibility, while the bottle, cork, pour, glass color, and guest reaction remain the decisive proof.

## Hook Patterns

- "他一口都不肯吃，全家以为他又在挑事。"
- "办公室抽屉一打开，老板愣住了。"
- "她说只是普通夜宵，结果全宿舍都围过来了。"
- "离家三年，她第一次在宿舍吃到这个味道。"

## Example Routes

- Snack: everyone at the office hides the same snack in drawers; the hook is the boss opening a drawer full of identical packs.
- Regional food: a homesick student recreates a hometown taste in a dorm room and calls family after the first bite.
- Beverage: a tired commuter is visibly irritable all morning; one cold sip resets the scene and changes their social energy.

## Risks

- Do not rely only on adjectives like "好吃". Make the reaction and sensory proof visible.
- Keep food safety and health claims conservative.
- Avoid making characters cruel around food; food stories usually work better with warmth, secrecy, or playful competition.
- For wine and premium beverage scripts, avoid making the protagonist look too plain when the payoff is "体面", "品味", or "关键场合不掉面子"; otherwise the visual reversal feels underpowered.
