# Beauty And Personal Care

Use for skincare, makeup, fragrance, haircare, oral care, body care, grooming, personal hygiene, wellness-adjacent beauty, and salon-style products.

## Buying Psychology

- Users buy confidence, social ease, attractiveness, self-control, routine, visible transformation, or relief from embarrassment.
- Conflict often comes from being seen: reunion photos, dates, workplace lighting, close distance, group comparison, live camera, elevator mirror.
- Proof must be visually readable and ethically framed. Avoid shaming immutable features.

## Best Formulas

- **Status Reversal**: someone dismisses the protagonist's change; harsh light, close distance, or social reaction proves it.
- **Dream Fulfillment**: the user finally becomes photo-ready, date-ready, stage-ready, or "the version of myself I imagined".
- **Expert Backing**: useful for skincare routines, oral care, hair/scalp, sensitive claims.
- **Sympathetic Misunderstanding**: a character avoids intimacy or photos; the reveal is a solvable personal-care issue.

## Proof Forms

- Close-up under consistent lighting, before/after with same angle, touch/texture reaction, mirror confidence, group photo placement, date distance, hair movement, scent reaction.
- For fragrance, show social and memory reactions rather than trying to "show smell".
- For hygiene, imply the issue through behavior and social distance before proof; avoid humiliating the protagonist.

## Hook Patterns

- "同学聚会合照，她又站到了最后一排。"
- "她刚进电梯，对面女生突然盯着她看。"
- "他说是灯光问题，下一秒他们走进了便利店白灯下。"
- "约会前五分钟，她突然不敢靠近了。"

## Example Routes

- Skincare: a girl avoids reunion photos for years; after a routine transformation, she finally stands in the center of the group photo.
- Oral care: a salesperson loses confidence before a client meeting; close conversation after use becomes the proof.
- Haircare: a roommate says the good hair day is from filters; a windy street shot proves movement and shine.

## Risks

- Avoid body-shaming or cruel social humiliation.
- Keep efficacy claims supportable; do not imply medical treatment unless verified.
- Use consistent lighting and framing when claiming before/after.
