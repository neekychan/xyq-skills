# Classic Brand Film Cases

Use these cases as reference material for brand-film routes and Seedance 2.5 prompts. Do not copy plots, characters, exact scenes, protected slogans, celebrity likenesses, or brand-specific symbols. Extract the underlying production mechanism.

## How to Abstract a Case

For each reference case, convert it into:

```text
Brand problem:
Category tension:
Film mechanism:
Style reference:
Style keywords:
Brand/product role:
Reusable rule:
Do not copy:
```

The goal is to use cases as craft patterns, not as moodboard decoration.

## Dior J'adore Fragrance Films

Brand problem:

- Make fragrance feel like desire, ritual, and luxury mythology rather than a functional scent.
- Preserve long-term J'adore codes while refreshing the face, music, setting, and cultural energy.

Category tension:

- Fragrance cannot prove itself visually through function; it must become a symbolic world.
- The audience buys a feeling of radiance, transformation, sensuality, and rarefied self-image.

Film mechanism:

- Turn the product into a mythic object.
- Build a world of gold, skin, water, architecture, fabric, and slow ritual movement.
- Use celebrity or protagonist as a living embodiment of the fragrance, not just an endorser.

Style reference:

- Luxury myth, fashion sculpture, palace ritual, golden body cinema.

Style keywords:

- `金色皮肤光`, `雕塑化身体`, `宫殿尺度`, `水面反光`, `丝绸与金属`, `慢速推轨`, `仪式动作`, `女神化主体`

Brand/product role:

- The bottle is not a prop; it is the compressed symbol of the world.
- The product shape, gold tone, and material codes should echo through light, costume, architecture, and body movement before the packshot.

Reusable rule:

- For fragrance, premium beauty, jewelry, luxury travel, and high-end beverage brands, build a sensory myth around one material code and one ritual movement.
- Product proof is emotional and symbolic: the world changes because the product defines the atmosphere.

Do not copy:

- Dior's exact gold-world composition, celebrity identity, Versailles-specific imagery, J'adore bottle silhouette, or campaign line.

## Nike Just Do It / Dream Crazy

Brand problem:

- Keep a decades-old brand line culturally alive instead of treating it as heritage text.
- Make sport feel like belief, sacrifice, and social courage, not only performance.

Category tension:

- Athletes and ordinary people are judged before they win.
- The audience wants the feeling of defying external limits.

Film mechanism:

- Use a manifesto structure: external doubt -> embodied struggle -> scale expands -> belief line lands.
- Move between elite athletes and overlooked figures so the brand idea feels universal.
- Let the voiceover carry a moral position, but anchor it in specific bodies and actions.

Style reference:

- Sports manifesto, documentary montage, cultural anthem.

Style keywords:

- `黑白/低饱和纪实`, `运动身体特写`, `群像推进`, `旁白宣言`, `真实汗水`, `逆光训练`, `公共争议`, `高密度剪辑`

Brand/product role:

- Product is present as equipment and uniform, but the true product is the brand belief.
- The brand line is not a tagline at the end; it is the logic of the film.

Reusable rule:

- For sports, outdoor, youth, creator, or challenger brands, make the brand own a conflict sentence: what does the world say the audience cannot do, and how does the brand help them answer?
- The more abstract the belief, the more concrete the action images must be.

Do not copy:

- Nike's `Just Do It`, `Dream Crazy`, specific athletes, political controversy, or exact cadence of the voiceover.

## On Running: Soft Wins, Real Energy, Zone Dreamers, Shape of Dreams

Brand problem:

- Move beyond performance-running specs into premium sport lifestyle, community, and cultural identity.
- Make running feel modern, light, social, stylish, and emotionally flexible rather than only heroic suffering.

Category tension:

- Running culture can become performative, competitive, and self-serious.
- Younger urban audiences often want movement to feel like energy, community, style, and self-expression, not punishment.

Film mechanisms:

- `Soft Wins`: reframes running through softness, recovery, cushioning, lightness, and a gentler winning logic.
- `Real Energy`: ties product sensation to music, cadence, shared momentum, and urban community.
- `Zone Dreamers`: uses a fictional cinematic trailer structure to make movement feel like confidence, courage, and collective dreaming.
- `Shape of Dreams`: uses a director-led cinematic short and celebrity creative process to move On from running shoe into lifestyle design language.

Style reference:

- Modern sport lifestyle, music-led running culture, cinematic trailer, playful premium fashion-sport, creative-process film.

Style keywords:

- `低调城市能量`, `群体跑动`, `节拍推进`, `轻盈缓震`, `运动即生活方式`, `明星创作过程`, `梦境式转场`, `干净机能服装`

Brand/product role:

- Product performance remains the foundation, but the film sells the feeling the product returns to the body: softness, bounce, energy, freedom, dream state, or social belonging.
- The shoe can be shown through gait, rhythm, landing, rebound, and group motion rather than static product beauty.

Reusable rule:

- For premium sport lifestyle brands, do not default to pain, grit, and winning. Find the new emotional contract: soft can win, energy can be shared, movement can be stylish, and running can be a cultural scene.
- Pair product physics with a cultural medium such as music, dance, cinematic trailer, or creative process.

Do not copy:

- Zendaya's likeness, Spike Jonze authorship, On shoe designs, specific campaign titles, exact music partnerships, or proprietary visual assets.

## Apple: Think Different, 1984, Flock, Shot on iPhone

Brand problem:

- Make technology feel like human creativity, personal freedom, privacy, and cultural change rather than hardware specification.
- Keep product capability legible while preserving brand restraint and taste.

Category tension:

- Technology is often invisible, abstract, intimidating, or reduced to specs.
- Users want tools that make them more capable without feeling controlled, watched, or dehumanized.

Film mechanisms:

- `Think Different`: associates the brand with creators and cultural rebels through minimal product presence and a clear belief system.
- `1984`: uses dystopian cinema to make a computing launch feel like liberation from conformity.
- `Flock`: turns invisible browser tracking into a physical threat, making privacy instantly understandable.
- `Shot on iPhone`: lets user-created imagery become product proof, shifting the claim from specs to visible output.

Style reference:

- Minimalist brand manifesto, dystopian liberation, surreal tech threat, creator documentary, product-as-tool proof.

Style keywords:

- `极简构图`, `黑白人物档案`, `反乌托邦秩序`, `隐私威胁可视化`, `屏幕光`, `创作者现场`, `安静控制`, `产品能力即结果`

Brand/product role:

- The product is a tool, shield, lens, or creative enabler.
- Abstract capability must be made visible: privacy becomes being followed, creative power becomes finished work, ecosystem flow becomes effortless continuity.

Reusable rule:

- For consumer tech and AI, do not explain features first. Convert the feature into a felt human problem and a clear visual idea.
- The product should resolve the visual idea with one clear action or one unmistakable output.

Do not copy:

- Apple's exact campaign lines, product UI, logo behavior, 1984 imagery, Think Different historical figure montage, or Flock's bird metaphor.

## Cross-Case Patterns

### 1. Build From a Brand Asset, Then Refresh It

- Dior keeps gold, body, bottle, luxury ritual.
- Nike keeps the belief command.
- Apple keeps creator-first minimalism and human-centered technology.
- On keeps running performance but refreshes it through softness, energy, fashion, and community.

Rule:

- Identify the brand asset that should compound. Refresh the world around it, not the asset itself every time.

### 2. Make the Product Structural

- Dior's bottle condenses the myth.
- Nike's gear supports the body in proof moments.
- On's shoes translate physics into feeling and culture.
- Apple's product becomes a tool, shield, lens, or output machine.

Rule:

- If the product can be removed and the film still works unchanged, the concept is too generic.

### 3. Use Style Reference as Production Grammar

- Dior uses luxury myth.
- Nike uses manifesto and documentary montage.
- On uses music-led sport lifestyle and cinematic play.
- Apple uses minimalist manifesto or genre metaphor.

Rule:

- A style reference must decide lighting, color, motion, framing, texture, sound, and scene logic. It is not enough to say "like Dior" or "like Apple".

### 4. Compress the Film Into One Memory Sentence

- Luxury: this product creates a mythic self.
- Sport: external doubt becomes fuel.
- Sport lifestyle: movement gives back energy.
- Tech: invisible capability becomes visible control.

Rule:

- Before writing a prompt, write the sentence viewers should remember. If that sentence is generic, the film will be generic.

## Quick Case Routing

Use this selector when choosing a benchmark:

- Need luxury, sensuality, ritual, desire -> Dior J'adore pattern.
- Need courage, challenge, belief, cultural stance -> Nike manifesto pattern.
- Need premium sport lifestyle, community, lightness, music, urban movement -> On Running pattern.
- Need technology as creativity, privacy, control, invisible capability, creator tools -> Apple pattern.
- Need product proof without hard sell -> Apple Shot on iPhone or On product-feeling pattern.
- Need brand heritage refreshed for now -> Dior or Nike pattern.
